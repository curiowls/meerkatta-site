Field notes

From speech to a useful note · November 17, 2026

A correction is part of the thought.

You can change direction halfway through a sentence without starting the whole note again.

You begin with Thursday, remember the calendar, and say Friday. Then you return to a detail you forgot. Spoken thinking doesn't always arrive in reading order. That doesn't mean you needed a better script before you began.

The question for cleanup is what to do with those turns. Some replace an earlier detail. Others show that you haven't decided yet. Treating them all as noise can make a note look clearer while making it less true.

Keep the correction. Group the details.

Friday replaces Thursday. The speaker explicitly corrected the date. Keeping both as possible review days would miss that correction.

The repeated detail becomes one. The mobile layout still needs checking. Grouping it with the prototype's status makes the distinction easier to scan without inventing a task owner or deadline.

A cleaner note can still be undecided.

"Maybe" does real work. Sending the prototype to Maya is a possibility, not a commitment. Removing the uncertainty would turn an idea into a decision the speaker never made. Likewise, "feedback by noon would help" isn't the same as "feedback is due at noon."

This is the boundary I want MeerKatta to respect: help the thought read clearly without deciding what the person meant beyond what they said. The app's processing guidance calls for preserving meaning, intent, and certainty. That is the standard to check against, not a reason to skip review.

Review the meaning, not just the polish.

Check the words with consequences. Before keeping or sharing the text, look at names, dates, numbers, and small words such as "not," "might," and "must." Ask whether anything tentative became definite, or whether two separate points were joined incorrectly.

You don't have to speak like a finished document. But the finished note should remain something you recognize as your own thought.
