You don’t have to phrase it perfectly yet

A small practice for keeping an idea before you know where it belongs.

A thought often arrives before a sentence does. You know there is something worth keeping, but the act of choosing a title, finding a folder, or fixing the opening line can change what you were about to say.

Keep the reason, not just the topic.

“Research onboarding” is a label. “I noticed people understand the feature but don’t know when they would use it” gives your future self something to work with. A useful capture preserves the connection that made you stop.

Try three unfinished sentences: “I noticed…” “This matters because…” “I want to come back to…” You do not need to answer all three. One specific observation is enough.

Let the roughness do its job.

You can pause, restart, or change your mind. Review the resulting text before keeping it, especially names, numbers, and anything whose meaning depends on a small word. Clearer phrasing is useful only when the thought is still yours.

Give it a return point.

Capture alone can become another pile. Choose a moment you already have—sitting down at your desk, ending a walk, or planning tomorrow—and open one saved thought. Turn it into a question, a message, or one next action. You can also decide it no longer matters.

The goal is not to save everything. It is to lose fewer of the thoughts you would have wanted back.
