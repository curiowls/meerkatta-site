Field notes

Your words, with a little help · November 18, 2026

Keep the words you would actually use.

A note can become easier to read without becoming more formal.

You say, "Let's give this a little room." The edited version says, "We should allocate additional time for further consideration." Both point in a similar direction. But one sounds like a person you know. The other could have come from almost anyone.

That's a useful distinction for MeerKatta. Cleanup isn't only about what disappears. It's also about recognizing the words that were already doing their job.

Keep the familiar phrase.

The name stays specific. Ohlalarm is a product name, not an ordinary word to replace with a more familiar spelling.

The expression stays ordinary. "A little room" carries the speaker's way of describing the design. Removing the repeated phrase is enough. It doesn't need a more impressive substitute.

Give different kinds of context.

Vocabulary is about the words. MeerKatta's custom vocabulary lets you supply names and terms you use. Adding "Ohlalarm" gives the app a spelling reference; it doesn't explain your writing style.

Writing preferences are about expression. A preference such as "Keep my everyday wording; don't make it more formal" describes the kind of editing you want. MeerKatta lets you provide voice guidance separately from vocabulary. These are directions you supply, not a promise that the app already knows your personality.

Read it back as yourself.

A writing reference

A sentence-level check

In Write Like You Talk, Paul Graham suggests asking: "Is this the way I'd say this if I were talking to a friend?"

This is an essay about writing, not research on dictation or evidence of MeerKatta's performance.

For a work note, I would adapt that question to the person receiving it. Would you say this to this colleague? Some messages need formality. Others just need to be clear.

Choose how much polish you want.

MeerKatta offers Light, Balanced, and Heavy polish. Alongside your writing preferences, you can choose how much editing you want. More polish isn't automatically better; the useful level is the one that makes the text easier to use while still sounding like you.

Start with one preference. Choose one recurring change you don't want, then review a short capture. If a familiar expression has become something you wouldn't say, edit it back. The goal isn't to preserve every spoken word. It's to keep the ones that make the thought yours.

Source & further reading

Paul Graham, Write Like You Talk, October 2015. Practical writing advice; the application to reviewing voice notes here is our interpretation.
