Choose where the thought goes

Not every thought belongs in the reply.

Speak into the app when the words belong there. Keep a separate capture when they don't.

You're answering a question about a project. Halfway through, another idea arrives: perhaps the question keeps coming up because the handoff document is missing an example.

Those thoughts are related, but they have different jobs. One answers the person waiting for you. The other needs a little more thinking. Putting both into the reply can make a simple answer carry an unfinished proposal.

Choose the destination before speaking.

For words that belong here: on Mac, focus the destination's editable text field, hold your configured dictation hotkey, speak, and release. MeerKatta processes the speech for insertion there. Review it before sending.

For a thought to keep separately: move focus out of the email's editable field before using the hotkey. When no editable field is detected, MeerKatta routes the recording to capture instead. Review the result in its capture widget and save it for later.

The distinction is input focus, not an AI judgment about your intent. Saying "this is a note for later" while the cursor is in an email doesn't tell MeerKatta to change destinations. Try both routes with a short test; editable-field detection and insertion can vary between apps.

Keep the answer small without losing the idea.

Separate doesn't mean unimportant. The handoff idea may become more useful than the reply. It just doesn't need to become part of this conversation yet.

This is why direct dictation and separate capture belong together. One helps you finish what you're doing. The other gives the thought that arrived alongside it somewhere to wait.
