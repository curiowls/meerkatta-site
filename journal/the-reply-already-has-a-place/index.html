Mac dictation, in place

The reply already has a place.

Sometimes the useful shortcut is not having to move your words.

An email is open. You know your answer, and the cursor is waiting. Speaking it into a separate note would work, but then you have another small job: find the text, copy it, return to the email, and put it where it belonged in the first place.

This is what makes a dictation hotkey useful to me as a design choice. It connects speaking to the place you're already working. The reply doesn't need a temporary home.

Start where the words belong.

Put the cursor first. With MeerKatta set up on your Mac, click inside the email's reply field. Hold your configured dictation hotkey, speak, then release it. Wait for processing and the text to appear before continuing.

Complete the requested permissions during setup and try this once in the app you actually use. Insertion depends on a compatible editable field; a shortcut isn't a promise that every app behaves the same way.

Say the reply, not an instruction to write one.

For this example: Ohlalarm is in your custom vocabulary. You've asked MeerKatta to remove "basically" when it's an empty speech habit.

  1. 1. Hold and speak

    "Hi Maya um thanks for sending the Ohlalarm draft. Basically the opening looks good to me. Could we shorten the last paragraph a little? I can take another look tomorrow morning."

  2. 2. Release to insert
    Hold hotkey→Speak→Release
    Inserted at cursor

    Hi Maya,

    Thanks for sending the Ohlalarm draft. The opening looks good to me.

    Could we shorten the last paragraph a little? I can take another look tomorrow morning.

Illustrative examples, not recorded app output.

Less filler. "Um" is removed.

Your preference. "Basically" is removed here as an empty habit, not when it carries meaning.

Your vocabulary. Ohlalarm keeps the spelling you supplied.

The words are still yours. Cleanup separates the greeting and groups the message. The request and tomorrow-morning commitment stay as you said them.

Keep the last decision with you.

Read before sending. Check the name, the request, and the commitment to tomorrow morning. Edit anything that doesn't sound right. Inserting text is not sending an email.

You can type the next sentence. You can rewrite the greeting. Voice doesn't have to replace the keyboard to be useful; it can simply get this reply into the field where you were about to write it.
