Behind the product · November 14, 2026

Why a meerkat?

A small, attentive companion for thoughts that don't wait until you're ready to write.

A microphone tells you what a dictation tool does. A mascot can say something about how we want it to feel. For MeerKatta, I wanted a little curiosity and warmth alongside the practical job of turning speech into useful text.

Readiness without another demand.

The posture says something. Meerkats take turns standing lookout while others forage. That upright, attentive stance feels right for a tool you reach for when a thought arrives. [1]

The moment is usually small. A connection on a walk. The beginning of a reply between meetings. You shouldn't need a finished sentence before you can keep it. For MeerKatta, readiness means an easy way to start a capture, not an always-listening microphone.

Clearer words, still your voice.

There is more to a call than sound. Research into meerkat communication adds another connection: individual voices can be recognizable, even within the same kind of call.

Research lens

Recognizing who is speaking

In a playback study with eight wild meerkats, researchers played calls that made one individual seem to be in two places seconds apart. The animals responded more strongly than when the calls came from two different individuals, supporting individual recognition by voice. [2]

This is evidence about meerkats, not a test of dictation software. It informs the metaphor, not a claim about MeerKatta's accuracy.

The connection is a design principle. Recognizing a voice acoustically and preserving someone's written expression are different things. But the study brings a useful question into focus: what makes a voice feel like its owner? In our product, cleanup should make the thought easier to read without making everyone sound the same.

Clearer text should still feel like something you would say.

A little warmth in a practical tool.

Curiosity leaves room for roughness. Talking to a device can feel formal, especially when you're still working out the point. A curious face feels more appropriate than an expert waiting for a perfect answer. That's the character I want beside the work.

Capture is only the beginning. The larger direction is to help you catch a thought and come back when you have room to continue it. That belongs in a workday, but also on a walk or between the ordinary things that fill a day.

Our meerkat is a small reminder of that intention: stay curious, keep the thought, and leave yourself somewhere to begin again.
